# Existing blind holding condition

20 October 2026. **Existing holding is owner-reported; the added drive is unverified.**

The owner confirms that the existing blinds stay in place when the chain is
released. The design therefore plans to switch the motor off between moves
and retain the original holding mechanism. This report does not qualify the
added chain preload or independently establish the behavior of every installation.

The added chain wheel engages the existing bead-chain loop. It retains the
headrail, clutch, roller and fabric attachment. The CAD does not include an
independent brake, ratchet or rated self-locking gearbox.

The original blind must hold its fabric position without motor current,
electronic braking or the dump resistor. Added chain wrap and preload must
not release or defeat that holding action. None of the seven installations
has yet been checked with the added drive against this condition.

The future prototype plan must distinguish the original blind's holding
action from incidental motor or gearbox friction:

1. With the added drive disengaged and the original blind intact, observe
   holding at several heights after movement in each direction.
2. Repeat with the intended chain path and minimum functional preload, with
   motor power isolated and electronic braking unavailable.
3. With the fabric appropriately supported, separate the added drive from
   the chain and confirm that the original mechanism continues holding.
4. Use a controlled, instrumented setup to establish motion, regenerated
   voltage and chain tension after motor disable and thermal isolation of
   the dump load.
5. Record the result separately for each blind.

These are test objectives, not an approved loaded-test procedure or a
request for measurements while the owner is away. The setup, fabric support,
instrumentation and limits must be defined before applying loads.

If independent holding is absent, the design needs a qualified mechanical
holding solution or a different installation. A larger electrical dump alone
does not resolve continued energy release after its thermal cutoff opens.
See the [electrical energy review](../electrical/transient-review.md).
