# Assembly and occasional service candidate

These instructions describe the modeled sequence. They require a prototype build to establish actual hand access, wire flexibility, fastener fits and safe repeatability. Do not solder or independently separate carriers with cells fitted.

## Initial assembly

1. Prepare both empty carrier PCBs, their permanent insulated raw-group links and protected pigtail. Inspect polarity, continuity, insulation, tail height and strain relief before loading any cells. The current 5 mm opposed-board gap and dielectric barrier stay intact; no open-book raw-wire slack is assumed.
2. Use the separately reviewed loading process and fixture to fit the matched cell set. The older eight-piece fixture needs a fit review against this revised restraint before use. Close the retained cassette; raw links remain inaccessible under its shell and removable outlet guard.
3. Fit the controller in its frame and clips, and make the supported controller-side motor-tail terminations. Seat the motor inline housing in the integrated tray and retain it with the actual housing tie. Fit the motor-tail keeper/pad. The pack outlet uses the front jaw, adjustable backing comb and compliant silicone pad; establish controlled contact, then qualify pull resistance and insulation condition.
4. Connect the protected pack pigtail to the controller and install the closed cassette/controller as a joint module. Seat the cleats and same retaining lock screw. Connect the parked motor and encoder leads and dress them to the declared paths. The optional home harness is not yet a completed assembly.
5. Assemble the buttons separately into the removed cover using the sequence below. Install the cover with its four real front screws. USB remains accessible from below; the chain runs through the declared upper exit.

## Buttons: finite rigid assembly

Each button is one integral printed face/flange/stem. Insert it from the front through the Ø10.3 cover aperture; its Ø12 face stays outside. Hold both buttons 0.4 mm inward using the one-piece shared nest. From behind, approach the open-sided guide bar at Y+8.5/Z−16, move to Z−0.2, slide Y−8.5 onto both stems and seat Z+0.2. Fasten its two M2×5 countersunk screws and ordinary nuts, then remove the nest. The no-nest shortcut remains a failed comparison.

Load the specified captive M2 locking nuts through the side pockets and fit M2×8 socket adjusters with a front driver. The bounded screw-tip range is Z62.25–62.75; do not treat it as a safe switch-overtravel range. With the real switch, establish first actuation and verify full release, no unintended preload and return over repeated operation. Do not add glue to hide an unresolved fit. Optional cosmetic plugs and O-rings are installed only after adjustment; their small nonconductive pry access is modeled but retention/removal remains a prototype gate. For reversal, remove optional caps, use the nest, unfasten and reverse the guide-bar path.

## Joint module removal

1. Remove the four front cover screws and cover. Support loose parts; isolate normal power as required by the electrical procedure.
2. Cut and remove the replaceable motor-housing tie, keeping the connected pair seated in its tray. Withdraw and park the encoder connector through the separately checked path. If a future home harness is fitted, its disconnect/parking sequence needs its own acceptance first.
3. Use the longitudinal motor-latch access, withdraw the fixed motor half straight 8 mm, then follow its explicit parking path. The controller motor tail stays retained in its keeper; its solder joints are not a handling grip. This encoder-first, motor-second sequence matches the checked flexible-path states.
4. Release the single cassette lock. Keep the closed cassette and controller in their fixed relative pose: slide the joint module +Y2.5 mm to release the cleats, then pull +Z72 mm clear of the backplate and retained drive.
5. Support the joint module on a nonconductive bench support before releasing controller clips. The modeled bench controller translation is −Y8 mm, making the protected connector accessible; inspect cable slack and avoid transmitting pull to solder joints. Disconnect the protected pigtail deliberately. Independent board removal requires the separate empty-carrier/raw-link disconnection procedure.
6. Reverse the checked paths for installation, using a new housing tie. Confirm no trapped wire, released button, free chain path and retained wall fasteners before operation.

The cover-off latch/tool corridors, screw access, rigid extraction and sampled parking paths are distinct checks. A final-state empty space alone is not counted as proof of this sequence. Actual plug latch stroke, hand grip, screw torque and flexible cable behavior remain physical gates.

## Successor shell approach

The corrected shell halves approach the unchanged paired spacer/contents by a modeled straight 20 mm Z stroke on each side. Local capture recesses and outer seam bands avoid the former material collisions without requiring the temporary catches to deform during that shell stroke. This is geometric path evidence only; the older workshop fixture, loaded handling, empty-cassette catch release, real print tolerances and insulation still need coordinated physical qualification.
